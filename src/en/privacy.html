1. Summary

  • We collect no personal data through Sunset Talk. We run no server for it, and it includes no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • While the app is running, it announces your nickname, avatar, peer ID and local IP address to devices on the same Wi-Fi (section 2).
  • Messages go only to the other person's phone, end-to-end encrypted, and history is stored only on the two phones in the chat.
  • Delete all data in Settings wipes this phone's data in one step.

2. What the app announces on your Wi-Fi

To find other Sunset Talk users on the same network, the app sends local network announcements (mDNS/DNS-SD, called Bonjour on Apple devices). They never leave your router for the internet, but they are not encrypted, so every device on the same network can see them, including devices without Sunset Talk that use network tools.

  • Your nickname (up to a length limit) and avatar (one emoji picked from a list, not a photo)
  • Your peer ID: an identifier computed from the identity key the app creates on first launch. It is not tied to a phone number or account.
  • This phone's local (private) IPv4 addresses, the ports used for chats and invites, and the protocol version

The announcements continue while the app is running. If you don't want this information visible, don't open the app on a Wi-Fi network you don't trust.

3. What goes to the person you chat with

This goes directly to the other phone on the same Wi-Fi. None of it passes through us.

  • Invites: sending or receiving an invite shares your nickname and peer ID with the other phone. The chat starts only when they accept.
  • Connecting (handshake): both phones exchange nickname, peer ID, public key, random values, a timestamp and a list of supported features to create keys used only for this chat, then exchange a value confirming they derived the same keys. The 60-digit safety number on screen is computed by each phone from the two public keys; it is never sent.
  • Messages: the text is end-to-end encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and only decrypted on the other phone. Message IDs, sequence numbers, send times, integrity hashes, delivery receipts, typing indicators and keep-alive signals are not encrypted, so someone watching traffic on the same network cannot read your messages but can see this information and the size and timing of data.
  • Reconnecting: messages missed while disconnected are re-encrypted with the new connection's key and exchanged.

4. What is stored on your phone

  • Profile and keys: nickname, avatar, peer ID, identity key pair and the device key that encrypts your chat history are kept in Android Keystore-backed encrypted storage, or on iPhone in the keychain (this device only, not synced to iCloud). Keys created with the person you chat with are not stored; they live in memory only while connected.
  • Chat history: kept in the app's own database. Message text is encrypted with this phone's device key. Details such as the other person's nickname and public key, message times and delivery status are kept unencrypted in the same database.
  • Settings records: safety number checks, block list, reports (up to 100) and your acceptance of the terms are kept in the app's settings storage. Reports stay on this phone unless you email them yourself.
  • Diagnostic log: the last 500 log lines are kept in memory only and disappear when the app closes. They leave the app only if you copy them from the diagnostics screen, and values such as parts of IP addresses, peer IDs and safety numbers are masked by default.
  • Backups: on Android, app data is excluded from cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.

5. What we receive

  • Nothing through the app. With no server, no accounts and no analytics, we never receive your messages, nickname, contacts or usage.
  • App stores: installs and updates are handled by Google Play and the App Store under their own policies. We only see aggregate statistics such as install counts that the stores provide.
  • Email: if you write to {{email}} with a question, a report or a launch-notice request, we use your address and message only to reply and handle the request. Email is processed by Google (Gmail). We delete it promptly once handled, and launch-notice requests after the launch notice is sent.

6. Permissions

Android

  • Internet (INTERNET): needed to connect directly to the other phone on the same Wi-Fi. The app does not connect to servers on the internet.
  • Network and Wi-Fi state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE, CHANGE_WIFI_STATE): to check the Wi-Fi connection and find people on the local network.
  • Multicast reception (CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE): to receive local network announcements (mDNS) on the same Wi-Fi.

These are normal permissions granted at install time, so the app never asks for them. No location or nearby-devices permission is declared.

iPhone

  • Local Network: required to find and connect to devices on the same Wi-Fi. If it's off, the app can't find anyone.

The app does not access contacts, photos, camera, microphone, location data or advertising IDs.

7. Retention and deletion

  • Information stays on this phone until you delete it. We hold none of it.
  • Settings › Delete all data removes the profile, encryption keys, chat history, block list, safety number checks and your terms acceptance from this phone.
  • Uninstalling: on Android, app data is removed with the app. On iPhone, keys and profile in the keychain can survive uninstalling, so run Delete all data first.
  • History on the other person's phone has to be deleted there. We can't reach any phone's data, so we can't delete it for you.

8. Sharing and international transfers

Because we receive no information through the app, there is nothing we share with third parties, outsource or transfer abroad. Email is processed by Google (Gmail).

10. Children

Sunset Talk is not made for children under 14. We collect no personal data from anyone, children included.
